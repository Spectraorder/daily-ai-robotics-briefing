# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — November 16, 2026

Monday's edition opens with an open-weight reasoning model that learns to use real robots from simulation alone, a survey that maps the whole VLA field, a five-tonne loader balancing on two wheels, a flywheel that lets a language model and a VLA teach each other, and a new test for whether world-model latents really encode actions — plus Tesla betting a car factory on Optimus, Huawei's open world model, and CoRL's best paper.

## Papers

**Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots**

Frontier robot-use agents run on proprietary models like GPT-6 Astra; open-weight models lag far behind. PEAR (a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics) trains an open 27B reasoning model with robot-use reinforcement learning entirely in simulation — no demonstrations, no harness — observing the scene, reasoning in chain-of-thought, and outputting the next end-effector pose. It jumps from 1.6% to 74.4% on LIBERO, near GPT-6 Astra at maximum effort, and completes 60% of trials on a real dual-arm robot it never trained on, where the base model scores zero.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562) · [Code](https://github.com/mit-han-lab/pear)

**Vision-Language-Action Models: A Life-Cycle Survey of Design, Training and Deployment**

Four years after RT-2, the VLA literature finally gets a field guide: this survey reviews 88 works and organizes them along seven life-cycle axes — inputs, architecture, action output, intermediate predictions, pretraining data, post-training, and real-time execution — comparing 22 leading models on data, size, speed, and hardware. Three trends stand out: training data has broadened from teleoperation to tens of thousands of hours of human video, model sizes have split between 3–7B generalists and sub-1B models that run on consumer GPUs, and generalist VLAs now span manipulation and navigation. It also flags that some VLAs with near-perfect benchmark scores fail when an object or the camera moves slightly.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18372)

**Heavy Metal Acrobatics: Learning Dynamic Control for Skid-Steer Loaders**

A five-tonne John Deere skid-steer loader doing a wheelie sounds like a stunt — until you realize only a handful of elite human operators have ever done it. ETH Zürich, Intel, and Blue River trained RL policies in simulation (with a learned actuator model and domain randomization) and transferred them to the real machine, which swings up onto two wheels, drives over rough terrain while balancing, spins in place, and lands softly back on four wheels. The work, published in Science Robotics, shows sim-to-real RL reaching heavy hydraulic machinery at the edge of its actuation limits.

**ESI: An Embodied Self-Improvement Flywheel for LLM–VLA Agents**

Bolting a reasoning LLM onto a VLA policy doesn't automatically produce teamwork: the VLA can ignore the LLM's subtask or undo its corrections. ESI treats the LLM–VLA pair as one unit of post-training — a flywheel that refines the LLM's prompts, a shared skill catalog, and the VLA's weights from deployment experience, keeping useful behavior even from failed episodes. On ten RoboDojo tasks it reaches 49.4% mean success, beating every compared hybrid agent and code-based method, including 10 of 20 on a packing task where GPT-6 Astra scores 0%.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18176) · [Code](https://github.com/Embodied-Self-Improvement/ESI)

**Beyond Prediction Gain: LASR as a Metric for Action-Grounded Latents in World Models**

A world-model latent that improves future prediction might just be memorizing the current state, the scene, or how far along an episode is — not the action. LASR (Latent Action Support Ratio) tests the latent properly: on held-out data, it compares prediction with the latent against state-only and non-action control predictors, summarizing typical support (GeoLASR) and the weakest case (WorstLASR). On robot manipulation data it detects known action information, rejects the shortcut controls, and grades imperfect latents — giving the field a principled way to audit what its latent-action models actually learned.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17775)

## Hot Discussions

**Tesla will end the Model S and X to build Optimus robots**

Elon Musk told investors Tesla will discontinue its Model S and Model X and convert their Fremont production space into an Optimus factory, with a long-term goal of a million humanoid units in that space. Early Optimus production is already underway at Fremont, an Optimus 3 reveal is expected in the coming months, and deliveries to outside customers are targeted toward the end of 2027. The move turns Tesla's humanoid bet from a side project into the company's manufacturing priority.

[Article](https://ia.ACS.org.au/article/2026/tesla-axes-series-s-and-x-to-build-optimus-robots.html)

**Huawei Cloud open-sources its Ascend-native world model**

Huawei Cloud launched and open-sourced CloudRobo-WM1.2 today, an embodied world model trained on its Ascend hardware, built around a shared world-model backbone and a DualAct architecture for action generation. Huawei claims it ranks first on RoboTwin 2.0 and LiberoPro 2.0 — vendor-reported figures, not independently verified — and the release brings a robotics world model to developers on China's domestic compute stack.

[Article](https://technode.com/2026/11/16/huawei-cloud-plans-to-open-source-its-ascend-based-world-model-for-robots/)

**SuperMimic takes CoRL's Best Paper**

Berkeley AI Research's SuperMimic won Best Conference Paper at CoRL 2026 for scaling humanoid whole-body control with a single differentiable world model. The headline number: a humanoid controller trained in 3 hours on one H100, against roughly 21,000 GPU-hours on 128 L40s for NVIDIA's SONIC. The award caps a CoRL week dominated by world models.

[Article](https://blockchain.news/ainews/supermimic-wins-corl-best-paper-for-differentiable-world-model)
