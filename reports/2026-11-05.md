# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — November 5, 2026

Thursday's edition is led by Apple and NVIDIA: HALO teaches a humanoid to move through real homes using only simulated vision-language-motion data, Cosmos turns world models into real-time interactive simulators, and a multi-lab study shows generalist robot policies can keep improving across tasks with the right RL recipe — plus a world model that predicts how objects should respond to contact, a tactile hand that feels its grip, robots that write their own code, and the first humanoid stock on the Nasdaq.

## Papers

**HALO: Perceptive Humanoid Motion from Simulation**

Apple's HALO sidesteps the humanoid data bottleneck entirely: instead of teleoperation, it synthesizes vision-language-motion data across a thousand procedurally generated homes and trains a three-rate stack — an orchestrator at ~0.5 Hz, a motion generator at ~3 Hz, and a tracker at 50 Hz — that transfers zero-shot to a real Unitree G1. It hits 91% success across 360 trials in six unmapped real homes, sitting on chairs, crouching under tables, and stepping sideways through half-open doors.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06908) · [Project page](https://apple-aiml-research.github.io/ml-halo/)

**Interactive World Simulation with Cosmos for Physical AI**

NVIDIA adapts Cosmos world foundation models into real-time, streaming interactive simulators with a single post-training recipe: Cosmos3-Nano-Sim-Bimanual turns robot and camera actions into the next visual observation, Sim-Transfer renders RGB from spatial controls such as depth and segmentation, and Sim-Auto generates synchronized multi-view driving video — a shared foundation for training and evaluating embodied agents in virtual worlds.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06627) · [Project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/cosmos-lab/cosmos-simulation) · [Code](https://github.com/nvidia/cosmos)

**Reinforcement Learning Enables Scaling Generalist Robot Policy Improvement**

Generalist robot policies usually need per-task adaptation after pretraining, and RL fine-tuning has mostly been studied one task at a time. This ETH, MPI, CMU, and UC Berkeley study scales online RL from 1 to 144 training tasks and finds critic-guided recipes — OGPO policy extraction and Best-of-N refinement — flip from forgetting to generalization, gaining up to 58 points on unseen scenes and 39 on unseen objects, while filtered supervised fine-tuning improves training tasks but generalizes far less.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06905) · [Project page](https://lasgroup.github.io/generalist-improvement) · [Code](https://github.com/lasgroup/generalist-improvement)

![Overview of the generalist-improvement protocol: rollouts pooled into a shared buffer, updated by FSFT, BoN, or OGPO, then evaluated on held-out tasks](https://lasgroup.github.io/generalist-improvement/static/figures/overview.svg)

**ReactWorld: Structuring Interaction Responses for Embodied Video Generation**

Embodied video world models often generate plausible-looking motion in which objects respond at the wrong time or place. ReactWorld predicts structured interaction responses — participant roles, response intensity, and interval motion — inside the generator and feeds them back to update object features and steer temporal attention, reaching 77.51 on RBench, 3.29 points above PhysisForcing, with gains in all nine categories.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06120) · [Project page](https://metaengine-world.github.io/ReactWorld/)

## Hot Discussions

**T-Rex gives robot hands a sense of touch**

Panasonic announced T-Rex (Tactile-Reactive Dexterous Manipulation) on November 5, built with UC Berkeley, NVIDIA, and Stanford: it folds high-frequency fingertip tactile feedback into a VLA so the robot corrects its grip in real time as contact changes, averaging 65% success across 12 delicate bimanual tasks — page turning, egg transfer, toothpaste, unlocking a door — 30 points above vision-only methods. It will be presented at CoRL in Austin next week.

[View post](https://news.panasonic.com/global/press/en261105-2)

**RAX: robots that write their own code**

A November 5 reel revisits Harvard-led RAX, in which a frozen coding agent explores on real robots, debugs its own mistakes, and compiles what works into reusable programs — no demonstrations, no teleoperation, no policy training. With more exploration budget, success on new scenes climbs from 25% to 79%, beating the prior state of the art of 41%.

[View post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfWwgctPRkv/) · [Launch post](https://x.com/du_yilun/status/2111143189222404427)

**Agility Robotics rings the Nasdaq bell**

Agility began trading today as AGLT, the first U.S. publicly listed pure-play humanoid robotics company, after closing a SPAC merger valuing it at $2.5 billion. The listing lands with thin financials — $1.8 million in 2025 revenue against a $138 million loss — but more than $300 million in committed multi-year orders for its next-generation Digit 5.
