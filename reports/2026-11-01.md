# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — November 1, 2026

Sunday's edition turns a robot arm into a drawing student, a human hand into a dexterous teacher, and a world model into a motion forecaster: a drawing benchmark that exposes how VLAs generalize, a tactile bridge from human demos to robot hands, a VLA that plans with world-model foresight, and a model that predicts video and 3D motion together — plus a reported Meta humanoid prototype and a daily robotics roundup.

## Papers

**BURGER: Benchmarking and Understanding Robotic Generalization via Embodied Drawing**

Instead of scoring manipulation only by success or failure, BURGER asks a robot arm to draw what language describes, starting from a blank canvas each time. Its prompt-driven pipeline generates 28,200 simulated demonstrations across 282 drawing tasks, and evaluations of five VLA models reveal distinct failure patterns in grounding, spatial composition, and multimodal action generation — including policies that generalize to unseen combinations of familiar objects but struggle with unseen categories. A Franka Panda deployment shows the approach working on a real robot, even under human intervention.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40532) · [Project page](https://siyingzhao.github.io/BURGER/)

**TouchBridge: Tactile-Conditioned Human-to-Robot Transfer for Dexterous Manipulation**

Human demonstrations are fast to collect but hard to transfer to robot hands, because matching joint poses alone can distort or break contact. TouchBridge's wearable TouchBridge-Cap glove and its Tac-Retarget method instead align measured fingertip contact patches and preserve the spatial relations among simultaneous contacts, with no object mesh, pose estimate, or simulation required. Policies fine-tuned only on converted human demonstrations reach 72.5% average success across four contact-rich real-world tasks, close to the 77.5% from teleoperated robot data, while human demo collection is 7.0× faster.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40349) · [Project page](https://touchbridge2026.github.io/)

**PRIME W0: Physical Priors from World Models for Embodied Manipulation**

Most VLAs act feed-forward from the current observation, discarding both long-horizon context and the layered structure of their vision-language backbone. PRIME W0 injects per-layer VLM key/value features into an adaptive mixture-of-experts action expert, conditions it on asynchronously predicted world-model subgoal images plus a DINOv2 history bridge, and generates actions with kinematics-aware flow matching. The result reports state-of-the-art performance on LIBERO and a real-robot benchmark, with ablations supporting each component.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39528)

**WorldFlow: Joint Video and 3D Motion Prediction via Large-Scale Pretraining**

Video world models usually leave 3D dynamics implicit, while motion predictors train on flow alone. WorldFlow jointly generates future video and query-based 3D trajectories with a unified Mixture-of-Transformers backbone, trained on 1.44 million object-level samples, and introduces the WorldFlowBench evaluation set. It reports the lowest displacement error among evaluated learning-based methods, cutting average displacement error on the SpatialCog set by 54.2% versus the strongest external baseline, and reaches 66.7% robot execution success on its real-robot tasks.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39278) · [Project page](https://worldflow-rbs.github.io/)

## Hot Discussions

**Meta's reported humanoid prototype, Max**

A report carried by Reuters on October 31 says Meta built a roughly 106-pound humanoid prototype, internally called Max, with Chinese startup Fourier in about three months, and has begun work on a second version. Meta is reported to describe the work as internal research with no plans to sell the units, and Reuters said it could not immediately verify the report — so treat the specs as single-source claims, not confirmed product details.

[View post](https://in.tradingview.com/news/seekingalpha:f12388378094b:0-meta-built-humanoid-prototype-in-robotics-push-the-information/)

**Why VLAs still fail, in five failure modes**

A carousel posted October 31 sums up the VLA generalization gap plainly: models think too slowly while the world moves, get confused by new rooms and lighting, let small mistakes snowball, lack enough robot data, and — crucially — mostly cannot feel, so grip is guessed from pixels. Its fixes are action chunking, more varied demos, recovery examples, shared datasets and simulation, and touch and force sensing.

[View post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfJda_flN3C/)

**KUAVO's eight-demo humanoid showcase**

A demo reel for Leju Robotics' fourth-generation KUAVO humanoid runs through voice dialogue, terrain-adaptive walking, VR-taught imitation learning that ends with the robot operating a juicer, coordinated hand-and-foot control, anti-disturbance walking, and vision grasping with tactile dexterous-hand perception — a compact tour of how far full-size humanoid demos have come as integrated systems rather than single skills.
