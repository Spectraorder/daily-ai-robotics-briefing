# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — November 22, 2026

Sunday's edition mines Friday's arXiv listing once more — arXiv publishes no new listing on Sundays — with five more strong papers: a tactile-innovation trick that teaches VLAs what vision can't explain, a diagnostic benchmark that separates thinking from doing, a 12,500-hour dataset of human intent, a reasoning model that outscores GPT-6 Astra on embodied tasks, and a compact world action model that loops instead of growing — plus a 552B-parameter VLA winning a plug-insertion duel and a real-time VLA catching a falling pen.

## Papers

**TacInno: Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models the Contact Dynamics Beyond Vision and Action**

Adding touch sensors to VLAs helps inconsistently, because much of what touch reports — like gel deformation as the gripper closes — is already predictable from vision and the commanded action. TacInno isolates only the residual, the "tactile innovation" vision and action can't explain, and uses it purely as a training target: a tokenizer compresses future tactile change into a discrete code, the policy learns to predict it, and all the extra machinery is discarded at deployment. TacInno-VLA averages 87.5% success across six simulated contact tasks and 70.5% across four real-world ones, beating the strongest visuo-tactile baseline by roughly 10 points on each.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26405) · [Project page](https://tacinno.github.io)

**Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation**

Most benchmarks can't tell you whether a robot failed because it reasoned badly, executed badly, or failed to connect the two. Mind2Act World splits 30 simulated bimanual manipulation tasks into three views — Mind (decisions from past observations), Act (precise, timely execution), and Mind2Act (closed-loop tasks where each physical outcome guides the next decision) — across 180 configurations, with a diagnosis harness that attributes each failure to one of the three. The gap is stark: the strongest agent scores 52.7% on Mind and 39.0% on Act, but only 12.0% when it must compose them, and eight tasks went unsolved across all 56,700 episodes.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26444) · [Code](https://github.com/Mind2Act/Mind2Act_World)

**HumanCOIN: A 12,500-Hour Dataset for Human Intent in Egocentric Manipulation**

Robot datasets record what a hand did, not what the person intended — yet intent leaves observable traces before the action: gaze lands on the target before the hand, muscle load builds before contact, cortical activity precedes the motor command. HumanCOIN records that pathway at scale: 12,500 hours of first-person manipulation across 38 scene classes, in three tiers — gaze inferred from headband video (10,000 hours), forearm muscle signals, and full lab-grade recordings with cortical activity. It turns intent, the missing state between observation and action, into trainable supervision for VLA models.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26434) · [Project page](https://flagopen.github.io/HumanCOIN) · [Code](https://github.com/FlagOpen/HumanCOIN)

![Instrumentation by tier in HumanCOIN: a four-camera headband for gaze, surface-EMG armbands for muscle load, and full lab instrumentation for cortical activity](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26434v1/fig4_instrumentation.png)

*Instrumentation by tier in HumanCOIN (Figure 3 from the paper).*

**ACE-Brain-0.7: Learning Execution-Grounded Reasoning for Embodied Agents**

Long-horizon embodied reasoning fails when a model's predictions drift from what actually happened during execution. ACE-Brain-0.7, a 9B vision-language model fine-tuned from Qwen3.5-9B, grounds reasoning in observed execution state: it was trained on 3.7 million question–answer pairs drawn from egocentric human video, robot episodes, and simulation, spanning task understanding, spatial grounding, and progress estimation. Across six public task-reasoning benchmarks it averages 76.5, ahead of GPT-6 Astra at 72.7 and Claude Opus 5.5 at 73.5.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26519)

![From egocentric video to annotation-derived questions: the ACE-Brain data pipeline turns a head-camera recording into question–answer pairs about task state and progress](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26519v1/fig_pipeline.png)

*The ACE-Brain data pipeline, from egocentric video to annotation-derived questions (Figure 2 from the paper).*

**LoopedWAM: Looped World Action Models for Elastic Inference**

World action models fuse video generation with action prediction, but their twin transformers are too heavy to deploy. LoopedWAM shrinks them by looping: each expert recurrently applies a shared six-block core, executing 30 transformer layers while storing only 12 blocks, and the video and action experts can run at different depths since visual features are computed once while actions are re-sampled. With only 0.58B policy parameters and no robot pretraining, it reaches 96.1% on LIBERO and 91.9% on RoboTwin 2.0 while using 7.4× less compute than the baselines — and elastic self-distillation trains one checkpoint that serves many inference budgets.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25595) · [Project page](https://anhdao69.github.io/LoopedWAM) · [Code](https://github.com/anhdao69/LoopedWAM)

## Hot Discussions

**ScaleVLA wins a plug-insertion duel**

A November 22 Threads demo from Galbot pits three models head-to-head on a real bimanual robot plugging a charger into a power strip: Cosmos-Reason2-2B and r0.5 both fail, while ScaleVLA — a 552B model using DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash as its brain — succeeds 9 times out of 10, versus 4 of 10 for the 2B model. (Caption in Chinese.)

[View post](https://www.threads.com/@agentoast.ai/post/DgC2IZch1oy)

**Humans are teaching robots — one headband iPhone at a time**

A November 22 reel walks through how VLA training data is actually collected: staffing firm Instawork reportedly equipped market vendors with headband-mounted iPhones, and Bloomberg reported similar programs recording factory and warehouse workers' actions for robotics companies. The egocentric footage is annotated into steps (reach, grasp, lift, move, place) to train VLAs, contrasting NVIDIA's GR00T N1.7 continuous-diffusion approach with OpenVLA's discrete action tokens — and raising fair-pay and consent questions for the data workers.

[View post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DgDZ8rfyGTJ/)

**A real-time VLA catches a falling pen in under 200 ms**

A November 21 Threads post reacts to a realtime-VLA demo in which a robot catches a falling pen in under 200 milliseconds from a 30 fps camera feed — reaction speed comparable to a blink. The catch: the benchmark ran on an RTX 4090 GPU, and latency grows with each added camera view, so the poster flags it as closer to practical than lab-only, but not yet deployable as-is.

[View post](https://www.threads.com/@datalign.ai.en/post/DgAcIZjEiDO)

**Teaching VLAs to stop — safely**

A November 21 reel breaks down "Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models to Stop Safely," the paper behind the SAFI method: when a VLA is told mid-task to stop a prohibited action, simply suppressing the task can leave the robot dropping whatever it holds. SAFI instead teaches a safe fallback — hold still, gripper steady — cutting the residual error rate to 1.3% without blocking the robot's other abilities. (Video captions in German.)

[View post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Df_8H9tk7rr/) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26239)
