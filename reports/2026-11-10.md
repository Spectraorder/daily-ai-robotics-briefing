# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — November 10, 2026

Tuesday's edition is dominated by launch day: DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2.5 puts humanoids on live Mercedes-Benz production lines, Boston Dynamics' Atlas trades its feet for scooter wheels, and today's arXiv crop brings humanoid VLAs trained on generated motion, latency-proof asynchronous control, and two big world-model scaling studies — plus a reality check on how many demos a VLA really needs.

## Papers

**DreamVLA: From Generated Human Motions to Humanoid Vision-Language-Action Policies**

Berkeley's team sidesteps the teleoperation bottleneck: generated human motion trajectories guide reinforcement learning in simulation, producing reliable task-execution trajectories that train a VLA — no new robot demos needed. A reference-conditioned DAgger step restarts the RL expert from failed learner states to fix the distribution shift left by offline imitation. On a Unitree G1 it handles picking, drawer-opening, button-pressing, and walk-and-pick in simulation.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10872) · [Project page](https://ember-center-berkeley.github.io/DreamVLA)

**Decoupled Asynchronous System for Humanoids (DASH): Latency-Agnostic Scaling of Vision-Language-Action Models for Humanoid Manipulation**

In closed-loop control, a big VLM backbone sits on the critical path — while it thinks, the robot keeps moving. DASH splits the policy into a slow VLM (System 2) and a fast flow-matching action head (System 1) running as concurrent processes, so the control rate is set by the action head alone. It holds a 20 Hz control rate from 0.8B to 8B backbones while synchronous inference falls from 9.7 to 4.1 Hz, with higher success at every size — and clearly better pick-and-place on a real dexterous humanoid.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11854)

![DASH architecture: a slow vision-language model and a fast action head run as concurrent processes](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11854v1/DASH_System_Overview.png)

**SWAM: Scaling World Action Models with Cross-Modal Alignment**

Adding action learning to a video-pretrained world model can damage its language–video alignment, so this team systematically studies the data and supervision recipe on a ~47,000-hour pool of general, egocentric, UMI, and robot video. Mixing egocentric video with UMI demonstrations improves control generalization, and the resulting model hits 88.1% (Easy) and 61.6% (Hard) on RoboTwin Clean and 40% across three real-world tasks versus 23.3% for the strongest baseline. Data, code, and weights are released.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12870) · [Code](https://github.com/shaaaaron/SWAM)

![SWAM world action model architecture: video and action branches connected through shared masked attention](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12870v1/model_architecture.png)

**ObjectWorld: Scaling Object-Centric World Models with A Large-Scale 3D Full-Object Flow Dataset**

World models need a representation that is both supervisable at scale and actionable for control. ObjectWorld proposes 3D full-object flow — dense 3D trajectories over a target object's complete surface, including occluded parts — backed by a 1,210-hour dataset built from real-world video and simulation with a scalable annotation pipeline. The resulting world model predicts full-object flow from an image and a task description, and drives manipulation across novel scenes and robot embodiments.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11085) · [Project page](https://nus-lins-lab.github.io/ObjectWorld/)

![ObjectWorld framework: predicting dense 3D full-object flow from an RGB-D observation and task instruction](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11085v1/Model_Pipeline_NoGoal.png)

**How Many Demonstrations Does a VLA Need? On the Sample Efficiency of VLA Finetuning**

Zero-shot and full-data scores hide what practitioners actually need to know: the adaptation cost. Across six VLAs (π0, π0.5, GR00T N1–N1.7) on four LIBERO suites, π0.5 needs 2–5× fewer demonstrations than π0 and is the most sample-efficient model at every budget, with the largest gap on long-horizon tasks; within GR00T, N1.5 adapts more efficiently than the stronger-zero-shot N1.6 and N1.7. Freezing the VLM during fine-tuning cripples the π models but barely touches GR00T — a pretraining difference worth knowing before choosing a base model.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09964)

## Hot Discussions

**Gemini Robotics 2.5 launches — DeepMind's most capable VLA yet**

Google DeepMind introduced Gemini Robotics 2.5 today: a reasoning model (Gemini Robotics ER) plans while the 2.5 VLA moves, across 200+ tasks with 30–100% higher success and 20–30% faster execution than prior baselines. The headline demo is industrial: Apptronik Apollo 2 humanoids sorted car parts on live Mercedes-Benz production lines at 96% success. DeepMind also released ASIMOV-Jailbreak, an open benchmark for disguised unsafe robot commands.

[View post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfkYjpcmeus/) · [Announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-5-accelerates-real-world-physical-intelligence/)

**Boston Dynamics puts Atlas on scooter wheels**

A November 9 post from Boston Dynamics shows the electric Atlas humanoid with its feet swapped for off-the-shelf scooter rims, landing a 360° spinning jump on the first hardware attempt — a research testbed for whole-body RL control at the actuator limits, not a product. The policy was trained in MuJoCo and adapted from Atlas's existing RL framework.

[View post](https://x.com/BostonDynamics/status/2119808355745071495) · [Blog post](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/atlas-on-a-roll/)

**A²-MEAS: fix the failure, don't just retry it**

A November 10 explainer walks through A²-MEAS, which treats failure recovery as a first-class problem: a MetaBrain supervisor diagnoses what went wrong and a learned "correction code" steers the frozen VLA's actions mid-task. On a real pour-over coffee routine, post-failure completion jumped from 14% to 94% on one step and 8% to 82% on another.

[View post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dfjewejj46v/) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09124)

**OpenVLA ignores the word "not"**

A November 9 safety explainer reports an unsettling result: told "do not put the bowl on the stove," OpenVLA still performed the forbidden action in 79% of runs — statistically the same as when asked to do it outright. Researchers localized the model's goal representation early in the network and steered it to change behavior in 12 of 18 runs, arguing safety checks belong outside the prompt, not inside it.

[View post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dfh3I3Akvcj/)
