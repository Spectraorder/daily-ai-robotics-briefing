# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — November 18, 2026

Wednesday's edition leads with agentic VLAs you can steer by geometry, a world-action model that finally puts its future predictions to work, a unified tracker-generator for manipulation, a humanoid policy that matches a foundation model with 75× fewer parameters, and a small VLA that survives rephrased instructions — plus Atlas heading to chip fabs, Xiaomi's factory trials, and a debate over how fast frontier models are compressing robotics research.

## Papers

**InteractVLA: Turning VLAs into Interactive Tools for Agentic Robotic Manipulation**

Agentic systems treat VLAs as opaque, language-only tools — InteractVLA opens a two-way geometric interface instead: the agent steers a frozen flow-based VLA toward an end-effector target at test time, then inspects the proposed motion to refine the next call. It reaches 99.4% on LIBERO-Plus, 48.62% on RoboDojo, and 89.4% on RoboCasa365, and its recursive self-improvement turns successful interactions into reusable strategies.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22584) · [Project page](https://lingwupro.github.io/InteractVLA/)

**RelayWAM: Making Future-Predictive Representations Actionable**

World-action models train on future prediction but throw it away at inference — and RelayWAM shows the action pathway barely reads those predictive features anyway. It adds a transition space of recurrent layers between the video and action backbones that relays step-relevant future cues into action attention, lifting LIBERO-Plus success from 51.50% (Fast-WAM) to 72.03% without generating future frames.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22994)

![Overview of RelayWAM and the representation–utilization gap](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22994v1/teaser_v2.png)

**TCP4D: A Unified Tool-Center-Point Tracking and Generation Model for Robot Manipulation**

One shared 4D scene representation does two jobs: the tracker recovers metric tool-center-point poses and gripper states from plain RGB video, and the generator rolls that representation forward into future TCP trajectories. It hits 7.60 mm trajectory error on DROID, 69.19% mean success on RoboTwin, and adapts to a new embodiment with ten demonstrations — plus a cross-embodiment pipeline that retargets recovered motion into training data for other robots.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23143) · [Project page](https://livioni.github.io/TCP4D/)

![The TCP4D architecture: a shared visual backbone and 4D features support metric geometry reconstruction, TCP tracking, and a language-conditioned trajectory generator](https://livioni.github.io/TCP4D/static/images/overview.webp)

**Body Schema Policy: Body as the Vision-Action Interface for Efficient Robot Loco-Manipulation**

Instead of scaling up, Body Schema Policy builds a body schema into the vision-action interface: joint and link tokens sit on the robot's kinematic tree, screw motions carry features between links, and each body part retrieves its own visual evidence. On nine simulated Unitree G1 whole-body tasks it matches the Ψ₀ humanoid foundation model with 75× fewer parameters and nearly doubles Diffusion Policy's success — and the same holds on a real G1 and a Fetch mobile manipulator.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22713)

![The Body Schema Policy architecture: joint, link, and global tokens initialized from state, denoising step, and action hypothesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22713v1/bsp_overview_C10.png)

**CoBoVLA: Decoupling Language from Action with Discrete Codes for Robust and Interpretable Robot Manipulation**

VLAs collapse when instructions are rephrased — existing models lose 22–52 points on LIBERO-Para. CoBoVLA splits the job: a text encoder maps instructions to learned discrete step codes, a visual planner turns them into action codes, and a language-independent actor executes. The 250M-parameter model scores 91.4% on LIBERO-Goal (vs 82.8% for SmolVLA) and 81.1% across 4,092 unseen paraphrases, at 17 ms per control step.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22395)

## Hot Discussions

**Atlas heads to the chip fab**

Boston Dynamics and GlobalFoundries announced November 17 that GF will be the first company outside the automotive industry to test the Atlas humanoid, with on-site trials at its fabs starting in early 2027; Spot inspection robots expand across GF sites in New York, Vermont, Dresden, and Singapore, after two units logged 90,000+ inspections at a 99% mission success rate.

[Announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261117426395/en/)

**Xiaomi's robots begin factory trials**

At the APEC CEO Summit on November 17, Lei Jun said Xiaomi's embodied robots have begun trials in its factories and can work an eight-hour shift while recharging themselves twice; the eight-hour demo footage itself dates to early November, and Xiaomi disclosed no robot counts, error rates, or deployment timeline.

[Article](https://asiadaily.org/news/19096/)

**How fast are frontier models compressing robotics research?**

A Humanoids Daily piece (November 16) traces an X exchange started by Tuesday Labs' Ros Shinkle — whole-body controller training fell from one week on 128 GPUs to three minutes on one GPU since SuperMimic — and Andrew Kang's reply crediting open tooling, world models, and LLMs automating the research loop, while flagging that most of the fastest gains are still in simulation.

[Article](https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/features/frontier-language-models-speeding-up-robotics-research)

**VLN-Flash circulates on Threads**

A November 17 Threads post shares VLN-Flash, a training-free decoder that scores a vision-and-language navigation policy's full action-prefix tree in a single forward pass, claiming a ~3× decoding speedup and up to 2.7-point success gains on navigation benchmarks.

[View post](https://www.threads.com/@sipirtu/post/Df1ypOEjg2U) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19287)
