# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — November 15, 2026

Sunday's edition mines Friday's arXiv listing once more — five more papers that didn't make the last two editions: code-as-policies that turn frontier-model reasoning into reusable robot programs, a world model that adapts when the dynamics change, a streaming action tokenizer for VLAs, a world action model that only imagines the future when it must, and a way to distill a planner's experience into a lone VLA — plus Runway open-sourcing Praxis-1 and OpenAI folding its robotics team into the main lab.

## Papers

**ACE-CaP-0: Real-World Code-as-Policies in the Era of Experience**

Frontier models can reason a robot through a task, but calling them at every step is slow — Code as Policies instead bakes that reasoning into executable programs the robot keeps. ACE-CaP-0 is the recipe for making those programs reliable: a coding agent revises policies with real-world feedback, requesting human demonstrations and simulation only when needed. Across eight real manipulation tasks — alignment, multi-object handling, bimanual coordination — the resulting frozen programs hit 92.1% success over 240 trials and run standalone in about one to two minutes per task, with no human or coding agent in the loop.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16872)

**AFTER: A World Model with Adaptive Fast–Slow Timescales for Error-Driven Retention**

World models break when the dynamics suddenly change — a grasped payload, a failing joint — because they can't tell a persistent change from a transient disturbance. AFTER-WM borrows predictive coding's trick of multiple timescales: prediction errors drive a fast correction that fades and a slow one that persists, reweighting frozen experts without retraining them. On five simulated robot datasets plus real UAV data it beats all ten compared models on multi-step error, cutting it 16–34% below the strongest baseline, and holds the lowest error 40 steps after a change.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15215)

**ActionCodec2: A Streaming Action Tokenizer**

Autoregressive VLAs tokenize motion into chunks that must decode in full before any action runs — the robot waits, then acts open-loop. ActionCodec2 is a non-neural streaming tokenizer that releases actions step by step while keeping reconstruction error inside a prescribed tolerance, then packs the primitives losslessly with an order-free Set-BPE. A universal version fitted on 20k hours across 24 embodiments averages 8.8 tokens per second, beats compared tokenizers on three simulation benchmarks and zero-shot on a real Franka, and lets policies use fresh state feedback inside a chunk — plus its tokens double as a readable control interface VLM agents can compose into reusable macros.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15490) · [Project page](https://zibindong.github.io/actioncodec2-page/)

**Bind-WAM: Binding-Uncertainty-Guided Selective Future Imagination in World Action Models**

World action models predict the visual future alongside actions, but generating that future at every replanning step is expensive and often unnecessary. Bind-WAM asks when imagination is actually needed: a lightweight router predicts language–object binding uncertainty — whether the current view supports a stable, task-relevant action — from the observation and instruction, and only then invokes joint future-action generation. In simulation and on real bimanual tasks, the router's uncertainty proves a strong, interpretable signal for when to imagine and when to act directly.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15368)

**What Can a VLA Learn from the Agent That Calls It? Task-Level Experience Distillation for Embodied Navigation**

Today's navigation agents have an LLM planner call a VLA every episode — costly and slow. H2E flips the distillation: instead of distilling the planner, it distills the agent's successful sessions into the VLA itself, relabeling every motion as a VLA action under the original instruction, masking loss on steps that move away from the goal, and marking states near the goal as stops. The 8B VLA then navigates alone at test time, lifting REVERIE-CE val-unseen success from 35.0% to 44.9% across all 3,189 episodes — and reaching goals the base model never found.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16246)

## Hot Discussions

**Runway open-sources Praxis-1, its 27B world action model**

Runway made Praxis-1 available to everyone with open weights under Apache 2.0 — a 27-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts diffusion transformer that predicts video and robot actions together, trained first on internet video and then on robot data. Runway claims the top world-action-model spot on NVIDIA's RoboLab-120 leaderboard at 46.2% success across 1,200 episodes, ahead of every open-weight VLA; the release includes weights, inference and fine-tuning code, and LeRobot support. All benchmark figures are Runway's own and not yet independently verified.

[View post](https://x.com/runwayml/status/2120533081182577116) · [Announcement](https://runway.com/news/company-news/praxis-1-is-now-available)

**OpenAI folds its stand-alone robotics team into the main lab**

Fortune reported on November 13 that OpenAI has reorganized its stand-alone robotics team, moving researchers into the main research organization and applied robotics staff into Applied; OpenAI confirmed the move and framed it as acceleration, not retreat — its general-purpose models, including GPT-6 Astra, can now drive robots directly. The evidence cited is an NVIDIA study in which an Astra-powered agent scored 74.8% on a simulated manipulation benchmark versus 51.0% for the best specialized model, though an OpenAI employee has pushed back on the "disbanded" framing. The 2027 humanoid demo target still stands.
