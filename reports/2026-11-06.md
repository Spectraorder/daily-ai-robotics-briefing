# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — November 6, 2026

Friday's edition is led by VLA self-correction and faster visual planning: UT Austin teaches a frozen VLA to recover from its failures with a handful of semantic corrections, NVIDIA makes visual planning roughly 25x faster, CMU and TRI model the world as moving 3D particles, and Berkeley gives a humanoid social reflexes — plus Xiaomi's humanoid factory shift, a first-person account of a robot cleaning an apartment, and LeRobot 0.7.0.

## Papers

**OopsieRecover: Learning Transferable VLA Corrections from Semantic Interventions**

UT Austin and TRI tackle the VLA failure problem without retraining: OopsieRecover infers the intent behind a handful of human interventions — via abduction — and encodes it as gated correction costs that steer a frozen VLA at test time. With only three to six corrections per task, real-robot task progress rises from 35% to 61%, and the learned corrections transfer zero-shot to new object poses, scenes, and related tasks.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08462) · [Project page](https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/oopsierecover)

**VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding**

NVIDIA's VFlash attacks a bottleneck in physical AI: VLMs that plan in visual primitives — boxes, trajectories, point motion — currently decode coordinates one token at a time. Its Flash Primitive Decoding predicts whole primitives in a single forward pass with a lightweight diffusion drafter, running about 25x faster on 3D point-motion forecasting and cutting latency ~80% on Jetson Thor and DGX Spark, while a world action model conditioned on its plans reaches 70.2% on MolmoSpaces versus 62.6% for the baseline.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08449) · [Project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/lpr/vflash/) · [Code](https://github.com/NVlabs/Eagle/tree/main/Embodied)

![The VFlash framework: parallel decoding of visual primitives for physical AI tasks](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08449v1/vflash_generalist_nvidia.v8.colors.v2.png)

*The VFlash framework (Figure 1 of the paper).*

**GPWM: Modeling the World as 3D Particle Motion**

CMU and TRI's GPWM is a world model that skips pixels entirely: it predicts how a 3D scene evolves by generating the motion of particles and rigid bodies, covering rigid, deformable, cloth, and granular materials in one architecture. Trained on the 25.5-million-frame ParticleWorlds corpus, it cuts prediction error by 31% on average against the best prior baseline, and it plans contact-rich pushing and picking by searching the model's latent noise for interactions that reach a goal — no reference trajectories or task-specific policies needed.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08338) · [Project page](https://robertren1122.github.io/gpwm/)

**Social Humanoid: Socially Responsive Human–Humanoid Interaction**

UC Berkeley's Social Humanoid gives a Unitree G1 something most humanoids lack: social reflexes. A shared-codebook VQ-VAE tokenizes human and humanoid motion into one vocabulary, and a Diffusion Forcing Transformer generates the robot's response — handshakes, high-fives, hugs, waves — in real time from streaming RGB-D video, executed by a whole-body tracking controller. Training data comes from a new 260-hour human–humanoid interaction benchmark built by retargeting five motion-capture datasets. It will be presented at CoRL 2026.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08407) · [Project page](https://von31.github.io/social-humanoid/)

## Hot Discussions

**Xiaomi's humanoid works an 8-hour factory shift**

Xiaomi's November 3 X post shows its full-size humanoid sorting center-console side covers for 8 hours and 5 minutes at its EV factory, with two charging breaks and claimed self-correction of anomalies while coordinating with the line system. Treat the headline with care: the footage is sped up 100x, and Xiaomi disclosed no parts count, success rate, or intervention frequency.

[View post](https://x.com/XiaomiTech_/status/2117542298238222588)

**A humanoid cleaned a journalist's apartment**

MIT Technology Review's first-person account of Tau Robotics' cleaning service in San Francisco, published today: the four-and-a-half-foot robot wipes counters, folds T-shirts, and even reattaches its own fallen toilet-wand head — but most tasks are still remotely piloted, it climbs stairs with a "drunken stagger," and a dropped operator link once ended a visit early. Every cleaning doubles as training data for Tau's more autonomous successors.

[Read](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/06/1148090/i-watched-a-humanoid-robot-clean-my-apartment-it-felt-like-a-taste-of-the-future/)
