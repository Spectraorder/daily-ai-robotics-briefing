# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — November 25, 2026

Wednesday's edition leads with five fresh preprints from the November 25 arXiv listing — one policy trained across a million robot embodiments, a world-action model that skips generating the future, differentiable training for humanoid whole-body VLAs, a physics-informed world model for motion tracking, and a mirror test that breaks today's robot policies — plus XPENG's IRON humanoid debut at AI Day and two new podcast episodes.

## Papers

**OmniAct: One Policy, One Million Embodiments**

A single policy that controls diverse robots must adapt its behavior to each body, not just transfer task knowledge across them. OmniAct conditions directly on each robot's kinematics in the scene through a scene-grounded kinematic graph — joint positions, motion axes, and connectivity tied to the visual observation — and a flow-matching policy predicts relative joint motions for each node. Trained on trajectories generated for one million procedurally designed embodiments, it reaches 63.0% success on held-out embodiment-scene episodes (vs. 17.8% for π0.5 and 57.9% for the strongest baseline) and 64.7% against GPT-6 Astra's 41.1%, and nearly doubles the next-best method on a previously unseen real-world robot.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33158)

**iWAM: Efficient Action Prediction from World Models without Explicit Future Prediction**

World-action models lean on pretrained video generation, but generating future scenes is too slow for real-time control. iWAM shows the future need never be rendered: exploiting the reversible dynamics of flow-matching models, it inverts each demonstrated future into its latent noise vector and learns a bidirectional flow map so a single video-backbone evaluation supplies the conditioning features the action policy needs. Across simulated benchmarks and real robots, it recovers the generalization benefits of explicitly future-conditioned policies at an inference cost near methods that discard future generation entirely.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33141)

![Training iWAM: a frozen flow-map video model maps the demonstrated future to its noise endpoint, and the action model is learned from that endpoint and the current observation](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.33141v1/iwam_training.png)

*Training iWAM (Figure 4 from the paper).*

**DiffGR00T: Execution-Aware Differentiable Training for Humanoid Whole-Body Vision-Language-Action Models**

Hierarchical humanoid VLAs decouple motion generation from physical execution, so tracking and balance errors never train the generator. DiffGR00T couples GR00T N1.7 with SONIC, a pretrained whole-body behavior foundation model, and replaces the non-differentiable simulator with a learned action-conditioned world model — letting multi-step body-execution losses backpropagate through the frozen controller and the flow-matching sampler directly into the VLA action head. On four HumanoidArena simulation tasks it lifts average success by 12.5 points over the supervised base VLA, from 59.2% to 71.7%.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32665)

![DiffGR00T architecture: the GR00T N1.7 VLA action head is trained while the VLA backbone, SONIC decoder, and action-conditioned world model stay frozen](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.32665v1/figures/fig2_architecture.png)

*DiffGR00T architecture (Figure 2 from the paper).*

**DynaMimic: A Physics-Informed World Model for Humanoid Motion Tracking**

SuperMimic's differentiable world model learns physics implicitly, and errors around contact changes accumulate over imagined rollouts. DynaMimic makes the physics explicit: its network predicts a contact wrench at each candidate support link plus a residual generalized force, then computes the next state through closed-form Lagrangian forward dynamics. Across 60 full LAFAN1 motions it cuts mean per-joint position error by 18.6% relative to SuperMimic, and on a real A3 humanoid the learned policies perform floor recovery, a cartwheel, and a box vault.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32944)

![DynaMimic framework: the world model is trained to predict the next state from the current state and action, then the tracking policy is trained using that world model](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.32944v1/pipeline-policy-terrain-input.png)

*DynaMimic framework (Figure 2 from the paper).*

**Embodied AI Fails the Mirror Test: How a Simple Mirror Breaks Robot Policies**

Can today's robot policies tell a reflection from a real object? RoboDojo-Mirror evaluates five policies — VLAs, world-action models, and an agentic policy — under single-mirror, dual-mirror, and mirrored-tabletop conditions, and every mirror condition cuts success: average drops of 10.8 to 56.7 percentage points, reaching 62.5 points on the mirrored tabletop. Matched non-reflective panels show 50–88% of mirror-specific failures happen without any robot–panel contact, pointing to phantom targeting of reflected objects and mislocalization of real ones; real-robot runs with π0.5 and FastWAM show the same degradation.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33010) · [Code](https://github.com/iPPLab/robodojo-mirror)

![Overview of the evaluation protocol in RoboDojo-Mirror: policies are evaluated on matched task layouts under four scene conditions, with non-reflective panel controls for selected policies](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.33010v1/benchmark_construction_v6_libero_4_resized.png)

## Hot Discussions

**XPENG debuts IRON humanoid and Turing Physical AI Architecture at AI Day**

XPENG unveiled its next-generation IRON humanoid at AI Day 2026 in Guangzhou today: about 170 cm tall, 76 degrees of freedom with 22 in each hand, over 1,500 tactile sensing points, and three in-house Turing chips rated at up to 2,250 TOPS. Serial production starts January 1, 2027, with store-assistant training in Q1 and a public release targeted for Q2 2027. The software story is the Turing Physical AI Architecture (VLA 3.0), one framework spanning cognition, real-time decision-making, and physical execution across vehicles and humanoids — a day after XPENG's cross-embodiment WorldGPT announcement.

**The mirror test is making the rounds**

Yesterday's RoboDojo-Mirror paper is getting picked up: its Hugging Face paper page frames the finding plainly — mirrors make manipulation policies worse because they confuse reflections with real objects, not just because they collide with the mirror — and AI Weekly's writeup leads with the up-to-62.5-point success drop. The "phantom targeting" framing is the detail people are quoting.
