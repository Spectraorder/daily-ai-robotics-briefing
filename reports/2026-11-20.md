## Papers

**PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation**

VLA models handle single manipulation skills well but fumble long-horizon loco-manipulation, where timing, skill sequencing, and precision tangle together. PGX offloads the temporal and precision control to two training-free classical components — a predicate-based state machine that gates task stages and triggers recovery, and world-coordinate locking that keeps the robot spatially consistent — so the VLA only has to focus on stage-specific skills. Tested on a Unitree G1 humanoid across three simulated tasks and one real-world task, it improves stage progression and overall task success.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26596) · [Project page](https://kl-research.github.io/pgx/)

**TuringPAI: A Foundation for On-Device Physical AI**

Physical AI needs models that understand space and dynamics yet run on limited onboard compute. TuringPAI is a family of small models sharing one vision-language backbone: TuringPAI-VL (0.65B) for spatial understanding, TuringPAI-Drive for autonomous driving, and TuringPAI-Embodied for manipulation. The drive model generates trajectories at 12 Hz on a single NVIDIA Orin-X chip, and the manipulation model reaches 96.8% average success across the four LIBERO suites — a shared foundation for driving and manipulation under real on-device constraints.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25474)

**Fast-JEPA: Advancing the Success-Latency Frontier for Latent World Models**

Latent world models plan by rolling out candidate action sequences, but recursive rollouts are slow at test time. Fast-JEPA reorganizes predictive computation: it builds a goal-directed plan to guide search and directly predicts the outcomes of complete candidates, avoiding recursive state rollout during refinement. It reaches 96.50% average success across PushT, Reacher, Cube, and TwoRoom with 2.41× faster planning than INTACT, and the authors add DynaGoal, a benchmark for world-model planning under shifting conditions and execution deadlines.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25927) · [Code](https://github.com/Sofilyzia/Fast-JEPA)

**Learning What Actions Change: Action Effect Matching for Dynamics-Faithful World Models**

Today's world models render increasingly realistic video yet often ignore how small action changes alter what happens next — a problem for policy evaluation and model-based planning, where near-identical actions can lead to different outcomes. AFFECT, an auxiliary training objective, injects temporally correlated noise into a policy's actions and trains the world model so its generated videos respond to that noise the way recorded videos do. On DROID, the correlation between policy success in the world model and on the real robot rises from 0.47 to 0.79, and planning with AFFECT-trained models improves task success by up to 8.3%.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25980)

![Overview of AFFECT: frozen encoders compare recorded and generated futures under action perturbations so the world model learns the visual effects of actions](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.25980v1/fig_main_v2.png)

**One Prior, Two Dynamics: Repurposing Video Diffusion Transformers for Self-Supervised Latent Action Learning**

Latent action learning recovers action supervision from unlabeled video, but usually learns the dynamics from scratch. O²LaF instead repurposes one pretrained video diffusion transformer as the prior for both inverse dynamics (inferring latent actions from transitions) and forward dynamics (predicting their visual consequences), optimizing both end to end through a compact bottleneck. It sets a new state of the art in few-shot action probing across navigation and manipulation, enables zero-shot action transfer across visual contexts and embodiments, and extends to V-JEPA2-style predictive priors.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26617) · [Project page](https://showlab.github.io/o2laf/) · [Code](https://github.com/showlab/O2LaF)

**How to fine-tune a VLA, in four steps**

A November 20 Instagram explainer walks through fine-tuning a vision-language-action model for a specific robot: record demos, normalize action ranges, then choose between full fine-tuning and LoRA — citing OpenVLA results where LoRA trained 1.4% of weights and reached 68.2% success versus 69.7% for full fine-tuning, on one GPU instead of eight.

[View post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Df9xEPhD8aW/)

**Why a VLA needs a fast policy underneath**

Another November 20 explainer uses a robot dog to make the latency case: a large VLA takes about 594 ms per decision, while a shoved quadruped falls past its feet in roughly 400 ms — so a slow VLA plans where to go at 1–10 Hz while a fast policy underneath runs balance at 50 Hz.

[View post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Df8iHMaDmPO/)

**A robot dog walks 10 km through the Alps**

A November 19 Threads post from a Dutch tech outlet covers an ETH Zürich quadruped that walked 10.1 km through Alpine terrain with help from a language model, descending 1,220 m over rocks, gravel, and railway tracks — while noting the model takes about ten seconds to respond, so it is not yet a hiking buddy.
