# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — November 8, 2026

Sunday's edition mines the last arXiv listing before the weekend: a diffusion VLM that folds action into the backbone, a 5B spatial world-action model that dreams in metric depth, a spatial-alignment upgrade for π0.5, a training-free trick that prunes 85% of visual tokens, and a language-memory fix for VLAs that forget — plus a Tai Chi humanoid, a cautionary tale from Beijing, and the week's robotics chatter ahead of CoRL.

## Papers

**Concertina: Embodying Manipulation and Cognition in One Diffusion Vision-Language Model**

Most VLAs bolt an action mechanism onto a VLM — discrete action tokens, or a separate action expert — splitting manipulation from cognition. Concertina puts continuous trajectory representation and physical-coordinate supervision directly on a shared 4.46B diffusion VLM backbone, with action projections taking just 0.21% of parameters and no separate action generator. It scores 98.15% on LIBERO, 85.9% on the 10,030-task LIBERO-Plus, and 4.142 average chain length on CALVIN ABC-D.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07513) · [Code](https://github.com/lose4578/Concertina)

**Spatial-UMI-Dream: Scaling World Action Models with Metric Geometry for UMI Manipulation**

UMI's moving gripper cameras entangle camera and object motion, leaving the metric geometry precise manipulation needs implicit in RGB. Spatial-UMI-Dream is a 5B spatial world-action model pretrained on 605 hours of RGB-D UMI data that jointly predicts future RGB, fisheye-aligned metric depth, and actions. It reaches 79.1% in-domain success on the new twenty-task RoboTwin-UMI benchmark and 85.0% average success across six real-world tasks.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07229) · [Project page](https://weiduoyuan.com/spatial-umi-dream/)

![Spatial-UMI-Dream's model architecture and two-stage training](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07229v1/fig2_method.png)

*The Spatial-UMI-Dream architecture and two-stage training (Figure 2 from the paper).*

**SLA: Scaling Spatial-Language-Action Model via Continual Pre-training**

VLA foundations inherit 2D visual representations, and spatial supervision usually arrives only at task-specific fine-tuning. SLA, initialized from π0.5, continually pre-trains with alignment between intermediate visual embeddings and a frozen 3D perception model, using adaptive layer-selection gates and state dropout across heterogeneous robot datasets — with all auxiliary machinery training-only, so the inference architecture is unchanged.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08373) · [Code](https://github.com/OpenHelix-Team/Spatial-Forcing/tree/master/SLA)

**T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models**

Every VLA decision processes hundreds of visual tokens, many of them background. T3R prunes spatially irrelevant patches via segmentation-guided masking, then re-biases attention toward action-critical regions and the instruction — no training required. On LIBERO-Spatial it drops 85% of scene-camera tokens while matching the unpruned success rate, adds up to 7 points under object-placement shifts, and reaches up to 3.3× speedup at higher resolutions.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08413)

![The two-stage T3R pipeline: segmentation-guided token pruning, then saliency-guided attention biasing](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08413v1/figs/fig_pipeline.png)

*The two-stage T3R pipeline (Figure 1 from the paper).*

**HeLM: Hierarchical Explicit Language Memory for Non-Markovian Manipulation**

Most VLAs map only the current observation to an action, so they fail at tasks that need working memory — counting presses, doing steps in order, remembering what happened last episode. HeLM gives a VLM high-level policy an explicit language memory, updated only when a task-relevant event is detected, while a VLA low-level policy executes. It lifts intra-episode success by 12.8 points on 10 real-world non-Markovian tasks, uses up to 4.5× less memory, and runs 2.5× faster than a keyframe baseline at long horizons.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02660) · [Project page](https://kimspacehuni.github.io/HeLM/)
