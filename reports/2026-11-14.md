## Papers

**What Makes a VLA Reactive? A Study of Execution Strategies for Dynamic Manipulation**

VLA models predict chunks of actions, but when objects move those chunks go stale before they are executed. This KIT study varies prediction and execution horizons, synchronous vs. asynchronous inference, and chunk-continuity methods on π0.5 across two dynamic simulation benchmarks and three real-robot tasks. The best prediction horizon is about one second, and keeping successive chunks continuous beats merely cutting latency — on the conveyor task, asynchronous execution lifts success from ~20% to 60–75%, and up to ~93% with ABPolicy. Reactivity is a property of the execution pipeline as much as of the policy.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15491)

**Scaling VLA Serving Under Robotic Execution Constraints**

Hundreds of VLA-powered robots are already running in production, so serving them efficiently is now a systems problem. XInfer jointly schedules heterogeneous VLA computation against the evolving response deadlines of robotic execution — reusing conditioning state across denoising steps and protecting time-critical feedback — and delivers 1.26–3.75× the effective robot capacity of state-of-the-art serving systems across three VLA models on production-derived workloads. Note: the paper's abstract points to a code repository, but that link did not resolve when checked, so no code link is included here.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14969)

**Does Your World Model Know Actions Have Consequences?**

World models are judged on reconstruction quality and task success, but neither measures whether the model predicts what happens when you take a *different* action. This paper argues for auditing world models by their sensitivity to counterfactual action perturbations, and shows that low state error and accurate sensitivity can trade off against each other — a model can look good on today's metrics while misjudging the consequences of its actions. A useful caution before deploying world models on hardware.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15048)

**Rethinking Actions: Continuity-Aware Priors for World-Model Planning**

Expert demonstrations have temporal structure — consecutive actions resemble each other — and this paper turns that into a planning prior. LG-DKGP learns a neural continuation of recent actions plus a Gaussian-process residual, proposes candidates around it, and lets a frozen visual world model evaluate them toward the goal. It reaches 87.07% macro success across five simulated tasks, against 71.03% for the best baseline, showing that better action proposals can matter as much as a better world model.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16645)

![The LG-DKGP method: a continuity-aware action prior proposes candidates for a frozen world model to evaluate](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16645v1/architecture.svg)

*The LG-DKGP architecture (Figure 3 from the paper).*

**JustRUA: A Controlled Study of GPT-6 Robot-Use Agents**

Frontier models can now drive robot arms through a harness, but reported RoboDojo scores differ in harness, test subset, and several design choices at once. JustRUA takes the designs apart in one framework — harness, action interface, experience reuse, scene geometry — and finds a lean harness that keeps full episode history and stops each motion early goes a long way: it lifts the RoboDojo score from 29.7 to 50.3 with no learned policy, no self-improvement loop, and no task-specific tuning, with scene geometry helping most.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16009) · [Code](https://github.com/ZefanW/JustRUA)

NVIDIA Robotics congratulated the RoboTTT team on winning the Best Student Paper Award at CoRL 2026. RoboTTT scales context for robot policies — more demonstration context at test time instead of more training — a fitting bookend to a CoRL week dominated by world models and VLA scaling debates.

**APS-VLA: dropping 75% of a VLA's visual tokens**

A short explainer covers APS-VLA, which trains a small token scorer on action supervision plus next-scene prediction to prune 75% of a VLA's visual tokens — the pruned π0.5 runs 1.47× faster on five real-robot tasks while holding success steady (60% vs. 61%), and OpenVLA-OFT keeps 95.75% of its LIBERO success at a 1.57× speedup.
