# Daily AI Robotics Briefing — October 27, 2026

Tuesday's arXiv crop centers on making robot brains faster, tougher, and cheaper to train: a force-reactive humanoid that knows how hard to press, a world action model that acts in a single denoising step, a sim-only agent that matches a frontier model on real hardware, a test-time adaptation trick that tunes world models for planning rather than prediction, and a 3D-splatting pipeline for precise wrist-camera reaching — plus humanoids in the wild, from a New York sidewalk to a martial-arts demo.

## Papers

**Learning Force-Reactive Humanoid Loco-Manipulation (FLoRe)**

FLoRe teaches a humanoid to control contact force as well as motion: robot-free demonstrations from force-sensing handheld grippers train a diffusion policy that outputs per-hand force commands, executed by a hybrid-compliance whole-body controller that tracks force along the task direction and yields along the others. On a Unitree G1 across three contact-rich tasks (board wiping, box rotation, gantry pushing), it succeeds in 80% of rollouts versus 26.7% for a position-only policy — force sensing, not just vision, is what makes contact tasks work.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35862) · [Project page](https://louis-leee.github.io/FLoRe/)

**OSWAM: An Effective World Action Model with One-Step Action Generation**

Direct-policy world action models skip future-video rollout but still need tens of denoising steps, too slow for real-time driving or closed-loop manipulation. OSWAM fixes the initialization instead of the sampler: compact "imagination tokens" from a pretrained Video DiT supply a structured action prior, decoded in a single Action DiT step with privileged-information distillation from a future-aware teacher. It matches multi-step WAMs at 90.3 PDMS on NAVSIM and 90.0% on RoboTwin 2.0, at roughly 3× the speed and as low as ~40 ms latency — one-step action generation without the usual quality collapse.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35850) · [Project page](https://gwenzhang.github.io/OSWAM)

**Pretraining Knows What, Simulation Teaches How: Compositional Generalization in a Sim-Trained Robot Agent (ZAP)**

ZAP splits the robot-learning problem in two: a pretrained 27B vision-language model supplies the "what" (reasoning, language, world knowledge), and simulation-only SFT plus GRPO on 40 simple tasks teaches it the "how" (motion), with zero real robot data. The result reaches 96.7% on real-world tasks — matching a frontier agent harness at 3× the speed and 1/17 the cost, beating a VLA baseline (33.3%) — and generalizes zero-shot to unseen objects, goals, and strategies like a mid-air handoff, drawing on a tic-tac-toe board, and playing chess, plus transfer to an unseen Franka arm.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35817)

**Adapt for Planning, Not Just Prediction: Optimistic Test-Time World Model Adaptation (OpTTA)**

Frozen latent world models degrade under distribution shift, and adapting them on prediction loss alone can leave them wrong about exactly the actions that would reach the goal. OpTTA adds an optimistic planning term: at each replanning step it minimizes prediction loss on observed transitions plus the planner's goal-reaching cost on its own imagined rollouts, biasing the model toward one under which the goal looks reachable. Across three JEPA world models and shifted manipulation, reaching, and navigation tasks, it lifts final planning success by 13.5 points over the frozen model, versus 6.9 for prediction-only adaptation — adapt for the planner's objective, not just the predictor's.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35636)

**Robot-Aligned 3D Gaussian Splatting for Precise Reaching with Wrist-Camera VLA Policies (PrecisionReach-GS)**

Precise reaching is a data problem: teleoperation is slow and full digital twins are heavy, especially for cluttered e-waste disassembly. PrecisionReach-GS reconstructs the real workspace once with 3D Gaussian Splatting, aligns it to the robot frame, and renders diverse wrist-camera observations paired with smooth reaching trajectories to fine-tune a VLA — no external camera needed. With 1,500 generated trajectories from under 10 minutes of human capture, π0.5 reaches 80.0% precise-reach success on motherboard targets versus 58.7% from 150 teleoperated demos.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35626) · [Project page](https://001-wang.github.io/PrecisionReach-GS) · [Code](https://github.com/001-Wang/PrecisionReach-GS)

## Hot Discussions

**An $89K humanoid practices its people skills on a New York sidewalk**

Footage from New York shows a humanoid (branded SFFR) strolling a Manhattan sidewalk, waving, flashing a peace sign, and forming a heart with its hands as passersby film it — framed as practice for future roles as babysitter, teacher, or companion. The reaction thread is notably skeptical, with many commenters questioning autonomy (a visible operator with a controller), safety, and whether childcare is a job robots should take — a reminder that public humanoid demos now spark a social debate, not just awe.

**Unitree's H2-X humanoid shows off martial-arts agility**

A demo reel of Unitree's new H2-X humanoid — about 170 cm, 65 kg, 31 degrees of freedom — shows it walking a stage, then outdoors performing flying kicks, jumping off a stack of crushed cars, lifting a sedan bumper, and flowing through martial-arts routines at night. Shown at a physical-AI event on October 22, it is the latest public flex in the humanoid agility race, where balance and whole-body athleticism are becoming the demo of record.

[View post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/De96UjOEnYA/)

**ISRO's robot astronaut Vyommitra previews India's crewed spaceflight path**

An animated explainer introduces Vyommitra, ISRO's first robot astronaut, set to fly aboard the Gaganyaan mission ahead of human astronauts to test life-support systems — monitoring temperature, pressure, and oxygen, and speaking in English and Hindi. It is a humanoid story of a different kind: a robot as a safety precursor, not a performer, in a program where the hardware must prove itself before people ride.
